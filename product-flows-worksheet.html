Case study 02

Product Flows Worksheet Redesign

The design file was the team's primary tool and "source of truth" for aligning clients and regional partners on payment authentication flows. Exploration of redesign was meant to accommodate future product scaling so the worksheet could better support different users: product teams, design teams, regional partners, go-to-market/sales, and external clients.

Role
Supporting UX Researcher
Methods
Generative stakeholder interviews · Thematic synthesis · Prototyping with Claude Code
Scale
n = 7
Output
Research deliverable and a working prototype of redesign with feature recommendations

The problem

The product and design teams maintained a design-file "flow worksheet" — a semi-static visual reference showing how payment passkey enrollment and authentication looked on mobile. It was the team's primary artifact for aligning on the experience flows.

The worksheet served multiple audiences, but the current structure and format could not adequately meet everyone's needs. Primary points of friction:

  • Teams had no visual reference for web, embedded, or responsive flows.
  • Each update to the flows required manual revision, so the worksheet was not able to immediately reflect and communicate new or upcoming changes to teams.
  • Sharing constraints either meant delays or required some teams to develop their own workaround for exporting and updating flows to showcase what they needed (primarily go-to-market and sales teams).

The product was preparing to scale — new regions, new locales, new authentication technologies. The team was concerned that the present format would not be able to support that growth.

My role

  • Supported lead researcher in scoping research objectives, drafting discussion guides (questions varied based on role), recruiting representative stakeholders, and running interviews
  • Synthesized interviews into themes and co-authored findings and deliverables into Figma exploratory presentation
  • My contribution focused on new features based on primary stakeholder's vision of worksheet enhancements
  • Designed and built prototype using Claude Code; lead researcher built similar prototype using Replit

From research to prototype

Seven interviews were conducted across the teams that relied on the worksheet most: go-to-market/sales teams, regional product teams, and product leadership. The goal was to understand how the worksheet was actually being used rather than how it was assumed to be used.

Qualitative data was synthesized using AI-assisted thematic analysis, and findings were presented in a Figma exploratory deck. The research surfaced a number of opportunities for improvement, which were then translated into a set of recommendations for redesign.

In addition, the research was carried into working prototypes: the lead researcher and I each built a prototype, exploring different variations of how the worksheet could be reimagined and restructured to meet the needs of its users. Mine was a single self-contained HTML file coded with Claude Code, where new features were traced back to needs expressed in the interviews.

What the prototype did

A fully self-contained single HTML file with no dependencies, covering five payment integration types:

  • Use-case, language, currency, and integration-type filtering — six languages, seven currencies
  • Multi-device preview across mobile, tablet, and desktop
  • Environment switching and a feature-toggle panel for beta methods
  • Design-file integration mapping real frames into flow slots
  • Export to document, vector, presentation, image, and video formats
  • Integrated developer documentation per flow
  • Full-flow overview and step-by-step demo views
  • Design intent annotations surfacing stakeholder quotes as tooltips

Each of those answers something the interviews surfaced: the filters and device previews close the responsive gap, the docked documentation removes a separate source to keep in sync, and the design-file integration attacks the maintenance cost at its source rather than adding another process on top of it.

Outcome

Both prototypes went to product leadership together — two directions from the same interviews, shown side by side. That turned the redesign into a choice between working options rather than an argument held in the abstract.

The one I built argued that the maintenance problem could be solved at its source, by mapping real design frames into the flow slots rather than layering another update process on top of the one already failing.

Conducted at a global payment network. Company and product names, participant details, and proprietary metrics have been omitted.
