Product Flows Worksheet Redesign

June 2026

A single design file was the team's source of truth for payment authentication flows — the artifact clients, regional partners, and internal teams all looked to for alignment. With the product about to scale into new regions and cover new authentication technologies, we explored what the worksheet would need to become to keep serving its users.

Supporting UX Researcher

Scale
n = 7
Output
Research deliverable and a working prototype of redesign with feature recommendations

The problem

The product and design teams maintained a design-file "flow worksheet" — a semi-static visual reference showing how payment passkey enrollment and authentication looked on mobile. It was the team's primary artifact for aligning on the experience flows.

The worksheet served multiple audiences, but its structure and format could not adequately meet everyone's needs. Primary points of friction:

  • Teams had no visual reference for web, embedded, or responsive flows.
  • Each update to the flows required manual revision, so the worksheet was not able to immediately reflect and communicate new or upcoming changes to teams.
  • Sharing constraints either meant delays or required some teams to develop their own workaround for exporting and updating flows to showcase what they needed (primarily go-to-market and sales teams).

The product was preparing to scale — new regions, new locales, new authentication technologies. The team was concerned that the present format would not be able to support that growth.

My role

  • Supported lead researcher in scoping research objectives, drafting discussion guides (questions varied based on role), recruiting representative stakeholders, and running interviews
  • Synthesized interviews into themes and co-authored findings and deliverables into Figma exploratory presentation
  • Developed new feature concepts from the friction points the interviews surfaced, shaped by the primary stakeholder's vision for the worksheet
  • Designed and built a prototype using Claude Code; lead researcher built a similar prototype using Replit

From research to prototype

The lead researcher and I interviewed seven stakeholders across the teams that relied on the worksheet most: go-to-market/sales teams, regional product teams, and product leadership. We wanted to understand how the worksheet was actually being used and where users were experiencing friction.

We synthesized the interviews into themes using AI-assisted thematic analysis, and presented the findings in a Figma exploratory deck. The interviews surfaced a number of opportunities for improvement, which we translated into a set of recommendations for redesign.

We also carried the research into working prototypes: the lead researcher built one in Replit, I built one using Claude Code. Each explored a different direction for how the worksheet could be restructured to meet the needs of its users.

How the work ran

Seven interviews carried through to two prototypes

What the prototype showcases

A fully self-contained single HTML file with no dependencies, covering five payment integration types:

  • Multi-device preview across mobile, tablet, and desktop
  • Use-case, language, currency, and integration-type filtering — six languages, seven currencies
  • Design-file integration mapping real frames into flow slots
  • Export to document, vector, presentation, image, and video formats
  • Environment switching and a feature-toggle panel for beta methods
  • Integrated developer documentation per flow
  • Full-flow overview and step-by-step demo views
  • Design intent annotations surfacing stakeholder quotes as tooltips

Each of these answers points of friction uncovered in the interviews: the device previews and filters close the responsive gap, the built-in developer documentation removes a separate source to keep in sync, and the design-file integration reduces the maintenance cost rather than adding another process.

Outcome

The research study was exploratory. The goal was to establish what challenges users faced when using the worksheet and what features could be added to improve the experience. Prototypes were built to demonstrate how the recommendations could be implemented and to provide a tangible artifact for stakeholders to provide feedback on.

Conducted at a global payment network. Company and product names, participant details, and proprietary metrics have been omitted.
