Case study 04

Security Animation at Checkout Survey

An A/B/C between-subjects test of two security animations against no animation at all, fielded simultaneously in two markets. The goal was to understand whether the animations increased users' trust in the checkout experience and their likelihood to complete a purchase.

Role
Lead UX Researcher · Research team with a research manager
Methods
Quantitative survey · A/B/C between-subjects · Significance testing
Scale
n = 1,800 · 900 per market · 300 per condition per market · 8-minute instrument
Markets
US and India · April 2025

The questions

  • Do checkout security animations improve perceived security, trustworthiness, and purchase likelihood versus no animation?
  • Which animation performs better — and does the answer differ by market?
  • Why do consumers choose one over the other?
  • Does prior familiarity with the underlying payment concept affect preference?

My role

  • Designed the A/B/C between-subjects instrument
  • Coordinated with brand, design, and product teams on stimulus development — specifically the three video conditions tested
  • Managed dual-market fielding with consistent quality controls across both panels
  • Shared the statistical significance testing across conditions with the team
  • Authored the comparative report and presented findings to the cross-functional team

Design

Three checkout flows compared in a between-subjects design. Each participant saw and rated one randomly assigned flow, then viewed all three side by side to state an overall preference — so the study captured both an uncontaminated first impression and a considered comparison.

  • A Branded token animation A branded payment-security icon appears during payment processing, representing the network's tokenization technology.
  • B Padlock animation A padlock closes during payment processing — a universal, symbol-based security indicator with no brand attached.
  • C No animation Standard checkout with no animated security indicator. The control.

The dual-market decision

The two markets were selected deliberately, not for convenience. The US and India have close to opposite baselines on familiarity with tokenization as a payments concept — the large majority of US respondents were unfamiliar with it, while the majority of India respondents already recognized it.

That contrast turns a confound into an instrument. If the branded animation depends on prior knowledge to work, running it in only one market would hide that; running it in both makes the dependency measurable.

Measures

  • Perceived security of the checkout experience
  • Trustworthiness of the checkout
  • Likelihood of future purchase
  • Animation appeal (animation conditions only)
  • Open-ended reasoning for the preference stated

Outcome

  • Animation investment justified. Both conditions outperformed no animation on security, trust, and repurchase intent in the US — meaningfully, not just directionally.
  • Padlock recommended for US launch. Its universal symbolism resonated regardless of prior knowledge, and it held its advantage even among the token-aware.
  • India is flexible. High familiarity and no significant winner opened the door to a market-differentiated strategy rather than a global mandate.
  • The branded token has an educational path. Its weakness in the US is an awareness story with a plausible trajectory, not a design failure.

Conducted at a global payment network. Company and product names, participant details, and proprietary metrics have been omitted.
